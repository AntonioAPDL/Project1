D. WIS explanation

The saved scorer sorts predictive values x_(s) and evaluates
  C_S = (2/S) sum_s rho_{s/(S+1)}(y-x_(s)).
For K=floor(S/2), alpha_k=2k/(S+1), l_k=x_(k), u_k=x_(S+1-k), pairing gives
  rho_{alpha_k/2}(y-l_k)+rho_{1-alpha_k/2}(y-u_k)
    = (alpha_k/2) IS_{alpha_k}(l_k,u_k;y).
For S=2K, C_S=(1/K) sum_k (alpha_k/2) IS_{alpha_k}; there is no median term.
For S=2K+1, C_S=[0.5|y-x_(K+1)|+sum_k (alpha_k/2) IS_{alpha_k}]/(K+0.5).

The odd form is conventional median-inclusive WIS. The even form is the
corresponding normalized weighted sum of interval scores. The principal
2,000-value representations and Gaussian 48/64-value representations are even;
raw GloFAS/NWS have 51/seven values. The interval levels therefore depend on S.
Use the central order statistic directly, avoiding a new median symbol m that
would overload existing article notation. This concerns the assigned rank grid,
not just the seven quantile-specific fits,
and does not turn the grid approximation into exact empirical-distribution CRPS.

Section 4.5 needs one qualified sentence defining weighted interval score (WIS),
with a reference to existing Appendix C.3 for normalization. Put the short
paired-loss identity and odd/even forms in C.3, before its existing empirical
CRPS discussion. Do not create another subsection, scoring table or score CSV.

Use Bracher et al. (2021), Evaluating Epidemic Forecasts in an Interval Format,
PLOS Computational Biology 17(2):e1008618,
https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618 . Its 2022 correction changes
illustrative values/figures, not these definitions. With the usual normalization
WIS approximates CRPS, not twice CRPS. Alpha is excluded tail probability;
central endpoints are q_(alpha/2), q_(1-alpha/2). Do not import the long metric
tables from the personal notes or score predictions against a new replicate.

Files: wileyNJD-APA.tex, appendices.tex, wileyNJD-APA.bib.
